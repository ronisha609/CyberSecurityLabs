# Foreign Recruitment & Insider Threat

By Ronisha Showers · Independent portfolio project


## Project Overview
This counterintelligence assessment examines how foreign state-linked remote IT-worker schemes can use false identities, U.S.-based facilitators, laptop farms, and trusted employee access to create insider risk.
## Scenario
A fictional U.S. technology company unknowingly hires a remote IT worker using a stolen identity. A U.S.-based facilitator receives the company laptop, establishes remote access, and helps conceal the worker's actual location. Documented DPRK remote IT-worker cases provide the real-world foundation; the company and its events are fictional.
## Assessment Focus
- Foreign recruitment and the role of witting or unwitting U.S.-based facilitators
- The pathway from false identity and hiring to trusted system access
- Behavioral, technical, personnel, and financial indicators
- Intent, capability, access, and organizational exposure
- Alternative explanations and intelligence gaps
- Detection, escalation, and mitigation recommendations
## Key Intelligence Questions
1. How does the operation obtain trusted access to a U.S. company?
2. How are witting or unwitting U.S.-based facilitators used?
3. Which combined indicators would raise concern without treating any single indicator as proof?
4. What information would HR, security, legal, finance, and management need to share?
5. Which controls could detect or interrupt the activity?
## Indicator Framework
| Indicator | Why it matters | Possible innocent explanation | Collection source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Laptop sent to an address unrelated to the employee | May indicate a facilitator or laptop farm | Temporary relocation or shared workspace | HR and shipping records |
| Unauthorized remote-access software | May allow an overseas person to control the device | Unapproved but legitimate troubleshooting | EDR and application logs |
| Sign-in location conflicts with the stated location | May indicate location concealment | VPN, travel, or inaccurate geolocation | Identity, VPN, and authentication logs |
| Identity or work history cannot be verified | May indicate a fabricated or stolen identity | Incomplete records or recent name change | HR and background-check records |
| Different person appears after hiring | May indicate an interview proxy | Video quality or appearance change | Interview and meeting records |
| Access exceeds the employee's job duties | May show collection beyond legitimate work | Poorly designed permissions | Repository, DLP, and access logs |
